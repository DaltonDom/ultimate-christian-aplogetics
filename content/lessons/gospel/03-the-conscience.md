A toddler who has been told not to touch the cookies is left alone in the kitchen for
two minutes. When his mother walks back in, he's standing very still with crumbs on his
face and his hands behind his back.

Nobody taught him to hide his hands. He just knew he'd done wrong, and he knew it before
anyone said a word.

That inner knowing is one of the most powerful allies you have in evangelism. The Bible
calls it the **conscience**. In the last lesson we learned to hold up the mirror of the
Ten Commandments. This lesson explains why that mirror works: there is already a witness
inside every person who agrees with what the mirror shows.

## The Law Written on the Heart

In Romans 2 Paul makes a remarkable claim. Gentiles (non-Jews) had never been given the
Law of Moses. They had no stone tablets and no Scriptures. Yet Paul says they still
**"by nature do what the law requires"** in many ways, because **"the work of the law is
written on their hearts."**

He then describes what goes on inside every person: their **conscience bears witness**,
and their thoughts **"accuse or even excuse them."**

You've felt this. After snapping at someone you love, part of you says *"That was
wrong,"* and another part answers *"Well, they deserved it."* That inner courtroom, with
accusation and excuse going back and forth, is the conscience at work.

Here is why this matters for evangelism. When you ask someone *"Have you ever lied?"*
you're not bringing them a strange idea from outside. You're **reminding them of
something God has already written inside them.**

## "Con-science": With Knowledge

The word **conscience** comes from Latin: *con* (with) + *scientia* (knowledge). It
means **"with knowledge"** or **"knowing together."** The Greek word the New Testament
uses has a similar sense.

Think of it this way. Your conscience is the part of you that **knows along with** God.
When you do wrong, there's a knowledge you share with Him: you know it, and He knows you
know it.

> Your conscience is like an inner witness in a courtroom. The person's mind may act as
> a clever lawyer and raise objections, but the witness has already seen what happened,
> and the witness is on your side.

## Speak to the Conscience, Not Just the Intellect

This is one of the most practical lessons in the whole course. Much of the time, when
someone raises an intellectual objection (*"What about evolution?" "The Bible has been
changed!"*), we feel we must win that argument before we can talk about sin.

Good answers matter, and the rest of this course will give you many. But notice the
difference between these two parts of a person:

- The **intellect** can argue forever. There is always another objection and another
  "but what about…"
- The **conscience** doesn't argue. It simply **knows**.

The Bible gives a vivid example. After King David committed adultery with Bathsheba and
arranged her husband's death, the prophet Nathan didn't start with an accusation. He told
David a story about a rich man who stole a poor man's only lamb (2 Samuel 12). David was
furious: *that man deserves to die!* Then Nathan said, **"You are the man."** David's
defences fell, because his own conscience had already passed the verdict.

So when a conversation gets stuck in endless arguments, it's often wise to answer
briefly and then gently turn to the conscience:

- *"That's a great question, and I'm happy to come back to it. Can I ask you a more
  personal one first?"*
- *"Even if we set that aside, have you ever told a lie?"*

You're not dodging. You're addressing the issue that matters most. Someone can argue
about the age of the earth for hours and still know in their heart that they've lied,
stolen and lusted.

Here are a few questions that speak straight to the conscience. Use them gently and one
at a time:

- *"When you've told a lie, did something inside you say it was wrong?"*
- *"Do you think you'll have to give an account for your life one day?"*
- *"If your conscience could speak right now, what do you think it would say?"*
- *"Have you ever done something you'd hate for anyone to find out about?"* (Don't ask
  them to tell you what it was. The point is for them to answer honestly to themselves.)

Notice that none of these is an accusation. Each one invites the person to listen to a
voice they already have.

## Why People Already Know Right from Wrong

Some people say morality is just a matter of opinion or culture. You don't need to win a
philosophy debate to answer them. Just notice how everyone actually **lives**:

- Nobody says *"that's just your opinion"* when their wallet is stolen.
- People from very different cultures agree that betrayal, cruelty to children and
  cowardice are wrong, even if they disagree on details.
- Even people who deny objective morality get angry about **injustice**, and they
  expect others to agree with them.

That's the conscience showing through. (Apologetics-3 explores the philosophical argument
from morality in more depth.)

Two honest qualifications will keep you from overstating the case:

1. **The conscience isn't infallible.** It can be poorly trained (making someone feel
   guilty over things that aren't sins) or distorted by culture. It is a **witness**,
   not the final **judge**. God's Word is the standard.
2. **Knowing isn't the same as obeying.** Knowing right from wrong doesn't make us good.
   It makes us **responsible**. That's Paul's point in Romans 2: because we know, we're
   without excuse.

## When the Conscience Is Seared

Paul warns of people "whose consciences are seared" (1 Timothy 4:2). **Seared** means
burned with a hot iron. When skin is badly burned, the scar tissue that forms loses
feeling. In the same way, a conscience that is ignored again and again can grow **numb**.
Paul describes people who have "become callous" in the same way (Ephesians 4:19).

This should make us careful in two directions:

- **Don't write people off.** Someone who laughs at the idea of sin may simply be putting
  up a defence. Often the conscience is alive underneath. Keep asking gentle questions and
  keep praying. Only God can open blind eyes, and He often uses a simple question.
- **Guard your own conscience.** Every time a Christian ignores a prick of conscience,
  it gets a little easier to ignore the next one. Keep short accounts with God. Confess
  quickly (1 John 1:9).

And here is the good news at the heart of it: the conscience can **accuse** us, but it
can't **cleanse** us. Only the blood of Christ can **"purify our conscience from dead
works"** (Hebrews 9:14). A guilty conscience isn't the end of the story. It's the
warning light that points to the only real repair.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise Romans 2:15** and explain "con-science = with knowledge" to someone this
   week.
2. **Practise the turn.** Write out two ways to say, "That's a good question. Can I ask
   you something more personal?" so it comes naturally.
3. **Read 2 Samuel 12:1-13** and notice how Nathan let David's conscience do the work.
4. **Examine your own conscience.** Is there anything you've been ignoring? Bring it to
   God today and receive His cleansing.
5. **Try the drills** and focus on asking about experience ("Have you ever been
   wronged?") rather than arguing theory.
