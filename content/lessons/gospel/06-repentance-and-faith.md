On the night of an earthquake in the Roman city of Philippi, a terrified jailer fell at
the feet of two prisoners and asked the most important question anyone can ask: *"Sirs,
what must I do to be saved?"* (Acts 16:30).

If someone asked you that question tomorrow, what would you say?

This module has built step by step. The Law shows us our sin, the conscience agrees,
Judgment Day is coming, and at the cross Jesus paid our fine and rose again. But good
news only helps someone if they **respond** to it. This lesson is about that response,
and about getting it right, because a wrong answer here can leave people falsely
confident or needlessly afraid.

## Two Sides of One Turning

Jesus began His public ministry with a simple call: *"The time is fulfilled, and the
kingdom of God is at hand; **repent and believe** in the gospel"* (Mark 1:15). Years
later Paul summed up his whole ministry the same way: *"repentance toward God and…
faith in our Lord Jesus Christ"* (Acts 20:21).

These aren't two separate steps where you do one and then later do the other. They are
**two sides of one turning**.

> Imagine walking the wrong way down a road toward a cliff. Someone calls out, and you
> turn around. Turning **away** from the cliff and turning **toward** safety are one
> movement. Repentance is turning from sin. Faith is turning to Christ. You can't do
> one without the other.

Sometimes the Bible mentions only one of them, for example *"believe in the Lord
Jesus"* (Acts 16:31) or *"repent therefore, and turn back"* (Acts 3:19). That's because
each one implies the other. True faith includes turning from sin, and true repentance
turns toward God.

## What Repentance Is (and Isn't)

The New Testament word for repentance, *metanoia*, means a **change of mind**. In the
Bible that change of mind always shows itself in a **change of direction**. You come to
see sin as God sees it, and you turn away from it.

It helps to be clear about what repentance is **not**:

- **It's not just feeling sorry.** Paul distinguishes "worldly grief", which is being
  sorry you got caught or suffered consequences, from "godly grief" that leads to real
  change (2 Corinthians 7:10). Tears can be part of repentance, but they aren't the
  whole of it.
- **It's not cleaning yourself up first.** No one gets well before going to the doctor.
  You come to God **as you are**, willing to turn, and He does the changing.
- **It's not sinless perfection.** Christians still struggle and stumble (1 John 1:8).
  Repentance changes which side of the war you're on. It doesn't end the war
  overnight.

And it isn't optional. Jesus said it plainly: *"unless you repent, you will all likewise
perish"* (Luke 13:3). He said this to people who assumed that disaster only struck
especially bad sinners. Everyone needs to repent.

## What Faith Is: Trusting, Not Just Believing About

Many people say, *"I believe in God"* or *"I believe Jesus existed."* That's good, but
it's not the same as saving faith. James points out that even the demons believe in God,
and it makes them shudder (James 2:19). There's a big difference between:

- **Believing *about*.** Agreeing that certain facts are true.
- **Believing *in*.** Personally **trusting** someone with your life.

Ray Comfort often uses a parachute to make this clear:

> You're on a plane that is about to crash, and you're handed a parachute. You can
> believe everything the instructor says about it. You can admire it and even hold it.
> None of that will save you. You're saved only when you **put it on and jump**, trusting
> your whole weight to it.

Faith in Christ is like that. It's **transferring your trust** from your own goodness,
religion or effort to Jesus and what He did on the cross.

It helps to listen for what people are **actually** trusting. Common answers include:

- *"I was baptised as a baby."*
- *"I go to church" or "I was raised Christian."*
- *"I prayed a prayer when I was a kid."*
- *"I try to be a good person."*

Some of these are good things. But none of them is a parachute. Saving faith means
letting go of every other hope and resting your whole weight on Jesus. A kind question
helps here: *"If God asked you why He should let you into heaven, what would you
say?"* The answer usually shows where someone's trust really lies.

Faith isn't a work that earns salvation. It's more like an **empty hand** that receives a
gift (Ephesians 2:8-9). Salvation is a gift. Faith is how we receive it.

## Calling People to Respond

When someone has understood the Law, the cross and the resurrection, don't stop short.
Tell them clearly what God calls them to do: **repent and trust Christ.** A few
guidelines (Evangelism-5 goes into more detail):

- **Be clear, not pushy.** Explain the response, but don't pressure anyone into saying
  words they don't mean. Only God can give new life (John 3:3-8).
- **Don't turn it into a formula.** A prayer can be a good way to express repentance
  and faith, but the words themselves aren't magic. What matters is a heart that is
  turning to God.
- **Encourage urgency.** Nobody is promised tomorrow. Many people find it helpful to be
  told, *"You can talk to God yourself, tonight."*
- **Give them something to read.** A Gospel of John or a Bible, and an offer to meet
  again.

## Assurance: You Can Know

God doesn't want His people living in constant fear, wondering whether they're saved.
John says he wrote his letter *"that you may know that you have eternal life"*
(1 John 5:13). He says we may **know**, not just hope.

Assurance usually rests on three things together:

1. **God's promises in Christ.** This is the foundation. Jesus paid it all, and God
   promises that everyone who comes to Him will not be cast out (John 6:37).
2. **A changed life.** 1 John gives signs of real faith: a new desire to obey God, love
   for other Christians, and continuing to trust in Jesus. These are evidence of new
   life, not ways of earning it.
3. **The Spirit's witness.** God's Spirit "bears witness with our spirit that we are
   children of God" (Romans 8:16).

> Picture two people crossing a frozen lake. One has strong confidence but walks on thin
> ice. The other is nervous but walks on ice a metre thick. Who gets across? The one on
> thick ice. It's not the strength of your faith that saves. It's the strength of the
> One you trust.

This is good news for tender consciences. If you're unsure whether you're saved, don't
spend years trying to reconstruct a past moment. Turn to Christ **today**, and keep
trusting Him.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise Mark 1:15**, Jesus' own summary of the right response.
2. **Practise the parachute illustration** until you can tell it in 30 seconds, then use
   it to explain the difference between believing about Jesus and trusting in Him.
3. **Write out your answer** to "What must I do to be saved?" in three sentences or
   fewer. Include both repentance and faith.
4. **Check your own heart.** Are you trusting Christ alone, not your record? If
   assurance is shaky, read 1 John this week in one or two sittings.
5. **Keep a few Gospels of John** in your bag or car so you always have something to give
   someone who is thinking things over.
