      has to be a gift is that we have nothing to pay with.

A young man stands in court, guilty of a serious crime. The fine is set at an amount he
could never pay, even if he worked for the rest of his life. The judge, whose integrity
the whole city respects, reads the verdict.

Then the judge does something no one expected. He steps down from the bench, takes off
his robe, stands next to the young man, and pays the entire fine himself.

The law has been upheld, the fine has been paid in full, and the guilty man walks out
free.

That's the heart of the gospel. The last four lessons were the bad news: we've broken
God's Law, our conscience agrees, and Judgment Day is coming. This lesson is about why
the **good news** is so good. As we saw in lesson 1, the Law is what makes grace
**amazing**.

## The Problem Only God Could Solve

At the centre of the Bible is a real tension. It's worth understanding, because it's the
question the cross answers:

- God is **just**. He cannot ignore sin, any more than a good judge can ignore crime.
- God is **loving**. He does not want sinners to perish (2 Peter 3:9).

So how can God forgive guilty people without becoming a corrupt judge? If He simply
forgives, He isn't just. If He simply punishes, we're all lost.

Paul gives God's answer in Romans 3:23-26. God put Jesus forward as a sacrifice so that
He could be **"just and the justifier"** of the one who trusts in Jesus. At the cross
**both** God's justice and His love are satisfied. Justice is done because sin is really
punished. Love is shown because God takes the punishment Himself.

## The Courtroom and the Fine

The courtroom illustration is one of the clearest tools you have. Here it is in its
simplest form:

> You're in court, guilty, facing a fine you can't pay. Someone steps in and pays it in
> full. Now the judge can **legally** let you go. You're not free because the judge
> ignored your crime. You're free because the fine has been paid.

This is what theologians call **substitution** (someone taking our place) and
**atonement** (making things right between us and God). Jesus took the penalty we
deserved, **in our place**.

Every illustration has limits, so keep two things in mind when you use this one:

1. **Jesus isn't an unwilling stranger.** He is God the Son, who laid down His life of
   His own accord (John 10:18). The Father, Son and Holy Spirit are united in the plan
   to save. It's the Judge Himself stepping down to pay.
2. **The payment isn't money.** Sin deserves death (Romans 6:23), and Jesus paid with
   His life.

**Why could only Jesus pay?** Someone might ask why God didn't accept payment from
anyone else. The substitute had to be **human**, to stand in the place of humans. He had
to be **sinless**, or He would owe His own debt and couldn't pay ours. And He had to be
**God**, to bear the full weight of the judgment and rise victorious. Only one person
has ever fitted all three: Jesus, fully God and fully man.

That's why Jesus' words on the cross, *"It is finished"* (John 19:30), are so
precious. The work was complete, and nothing is left for us to add. We don't pay
part of the fine and let Jesus cover the rest. He paid it all.

## The Great Exchange

2 Corinthians 5:21 may be the most compressed summary of the gospel in the Bible: *"For
our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the
righteousness of God."*

Two things happen:

- **Our sin was credited to Jesus.** He never sinned, but He was treated as the sinner
  and bore the punishment.
- **His righteousness is credited to us.** We haven't lived perfectly, but those who
  trust Him are treated as if we had.

Picture a bank account deep in debt. Grace doesn't just bring it back to zero. It fills
it with Christ's perfect record. We don't only escape punishment. We are **welcomed as
righteous**.

This was promised long before it happened. Centuries before Jesus (traditionally
about 700 years), Isaiah wrote
of a servant who would be **"pierced for our transgressions"** and **"crushed for our
iniquities"**, on whom **"the LORD has laid… the iniquity of us all"** (Isaiah 53:5-6).
Christians have always seen Jesus in that chapter, and the fit is striking.

## Love You Can Measure

People often say *"God loves you"*, and it's true. But how do we know? Romans 5:8
answers: *"God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died
for us."*

Notice **when** He died for us: not after we cleaned up, not once we were good, but
**while we were still sinners.** And notice **how** God showed His love. He didn't just
tell us. He paid a price.

Human love is measured by what it's willing to give up. On that measure the cross is the
greatest act of love in history. This is why the Law has to come first. Someone who
doesn't believe they're guilty will find the cross puzzling. Someone who knows they're
guilty finds it overwhelming.

## The Resurrection: God's Receipt

If Jesus had stayed in the tomb, how would we know His death achieved anything? He'd be
one more good man who died unjustly.

But Paul says Jesus *"was delivered up for our trespasses and raised for our
justification"* (Romans 4:25). The resurrection is God's public declaration that the
payment was **accepted**.

> When you pay a large bill, you keep the receipt. It's your proof that the debt is
> settled. The empty tomb is God's receipt. The debt is paid, and it will never be
> charged again.

That's why Paul says that if Christ hasn't been raised, our faith is futile and we're
still in our sins (1 Corinthians 15:17). The cross and the resurrection go together.
Always include the resurrection when you share the gospel. (Apologetics-6 covers the
evidence for it.)

## Telling It Simply

After someone has seen their guilt, here's a way to explain the cross in about a minute:

1. *"We've broken God's Law, and a good judge can't just ignore that."*
2. *"But God loved us so much that He became a man, Jesus, who lived the perfect life we
   haven't."*
3. *"On the cross He took the punishment we deserved. It's like He paid our fine."*
4. *"Three days later He rose from the dead, which proves the payment was accepted."*
5. *"Because the fine is paid, God can forgive us and still be completely just."*

Then pause and ask, **"Does that make sense?"** Don't hurry. Let it sink in. When
someone who has just seen their guilt really hears this, it often lands with real weight.
Give it room.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise 2 Corinthians 5:21** and practise explaining the great exchange in your
   own words.
2. **Tell the courtroom illustration** out loud three times this week, including the
   resurrection as God's receipt.
3. **Read Isaiah 53** in one sitting and note every phrase that describes substitution
   ("for our…", "laid on him").
4. **Write out the five-step explanation** above and trim it until you can say it
   naturally in under a minute.
5. **Spend time in worship.** Before you tell anyone else, let the cross amaze you again.
   It's hard to make grace sound amazing if it has become ordinary to you.
