Imagine opening your mail and finding a court summons with your name on it. The date is
set and attendance is compulsory. You don't know what day it is, only that it's coming.

Most of us would think of little else until the day came.

The Bible says every person has exactly that kind of appointment: *"it is appointed for
man to die once, and after that comes judgment"* (Hebrews 9:27). In the first lessons of
this module we saw how the Law and the conscience show people they're guilty. This lesson
answers the question that naturally follows: **so what?** What happens to guilty people?

## A Day Already on the Calendar

When Paul preached to the philosophers in Athens, he ended with a remarkable statement.
God **"has fixed a day on which he will judge the world in righteousness by a man whom
he has appointed; and of this he has given assurance to all by raising him from the
dead"** (Acts 17:31).

Look at what that verse tells us:

- **The day is fixed.** It isn't a vague possibility. It's on God's calendar.
- **The judgment will be righteous.** Nothing will be missed, and nothing will be
  exaggerated.
- **Jesus is the Judge.** The man God has appointed is Jesus Himself (John 5:22).
- **The resurrection is the proof.** God didn't ask us to take this on blind faith. He
  raised the Judge from the dead.

It's worth noticing that Christians didn't invent Judgment Day to frighten people. The
idea runs from the Old Testament prophets through Jesus to the apostles. And the
alternative is worse than it first sounds. A universe with no final judgment is a
universe where tyrants and abusers who escaped justice in this life simply get away with
it. Deep down, most people **want** justice. They just don't expect it to apply to them.

## What Judgment Day Will Be Like

In Revelation 20:11-15, John describes a vision of a **great white throne**. The dead,
"great and small", stand before it. Books are opened, and the dead are **judged by what
they have done**. Another book, the **book of life**, is opened too, and those not found
in it face the second death.

A few things stand out:

- **Everyone will be there.** Great and small, famous and forgotten. No one is too
  important or too insignificant.
- **Everything will be seen.** Romans 2:16 says God will judge "the secrets of men."
  Every thought and hidden motive will be brought into the open.
- **Judgment is by works, and that is the problem.** If we're judged by our record, the
  Ten Commandments have already shown us how it will go.
- **There's another book.** The book of life reminds us that there's hope. Some people
  are rescued, not because of their record, but because of the Lamb's (Revelation 21:27).

## Why Hell Exists

Many people are surprised to learn that **Jesus spoke about hell more than anyone else
in the Bible.** He didn't treat it as a figure of speech. He warned: *"do not fear those
who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. Rather fear him who can destroy both soul
and body in hell"* (Matthew 10:28).

Why would a good God allow such a place? Because He is **good**. Think about it:

- A judge who never punished crime would not be a good judge. He'd be corrupt.
- God's anger at sin isn't a bad temper. It's the settled opposition of perfect goodness
  to everything that destroys and defiles.
- Hell is, in the end, God giving people what they have chosen, which is life apart from
  Him. The same God who is the source of every good thing will no longer be there to
  give it.

Jesus used several vivid pictures for it: **"outer darkness"** where there is weeping
(Matthew 8:12), **fire that is not quenched** (Mark 9:43), and **"eternal punishment"**
(Matthew 25:46). Christians differ on how literally to take some of these images. Is it
physical fire, or fire as a picture of God's judgment? But no one should conclude that
the reality is milder than the pictures. Jesus chose these words to warn us, not to
reassure us.

Jesus also taught that judgment will be **exactly fair**. Those who knew more will be
held more responsible (Luke 12:47-48). No one will be punished for more than they have
done, and no one will be able to say God was unjust.

Why does hell seem too harsh to us? Often it's because we underestimate **how holy God
is** and **how serious sin is**.

> Kicking a football, kicking a dog, and kicking a person are the same action, but not
> the same wrong. The seriousness of an offence depends partly on who it's against. Sin
> is rebellion against an infinitely good and holy God.

## How to Speak About Hell

This is where many Christians go wrong in one of two ways. Some **never mention hell**,
because they're embarrassed or afraid. Others talk about it **harshly or even gleefully**,
as if they enjoy the idea of someone else getting what's coming to them. Both are
failures of love.

Look at the heart of God and of His people:

- God says, *"I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn
  from his way and live"* (Ezekiel 33:11).
- Jesus **wept** over Jerusalem as He foresaw its judgment (Luke 19:41).
- Paul warned people "with tears" night and day for three years (Acts 20:31).
- God is holding back judgment because He is **patient**, not wishing any to perish
  (2 Peter 3:9).

So when you speak of hell:

1. **Speak soberly.** This is the most serious subject there is. Don't joke, and don't
   use it as a clever opening line.
2. **Include yourself.** *"On my own record I'd deserve it too."* That's true, and it
   removes any hint of superiority.
3. **Don't speculate.** You don't need to describe hell in graphic detail or answer every
   question about it. Say what Jesus said, and say it plainly.
4. **Speak with love.** A warning is an act of love. A firefighter who shouts *"Get out!"*
   isn't being rude. He's trying to save your life.
5. **Always follow with the cross.** Never leave someone with the warning alone. The
   reason we speak of judgment is that there is a way of escape. Your next sentence
   should be about Jesus.

It's also worth praying for a softer heart. If the reality of hell doesn't move you,
ask God to help you feel what He feels for the people around you.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise Hebrews 9:27.** It's short, clear, and easy to bring into conversation.
2. **Read Revelation 20:11-15 and Luke 16:19-31** slowly and prayerfully. Let them
   sober you before you speak to anyone else.
3. **Practise your answer** to "Are you saying I'm going to hell?" Make sure it includes
   yourself and moves to the cross.
4. **Pray by name** for three people you know who aren't ready to face God. Ask God to
   give you His compassion for them.
5. **Read the "where Christians differ" section** for this lesson and be ready to explain, if asked, what faithful
   Christians agree on even where they disagree about the nature of hell.
