At some point, every pro-life Christian hears it: *"But what about rape?"* Or, *"What if the
mother will die?"* Or, *"Isn't it cruel to bring a severely disabled child into the world?"*
These are the **hard cases**, and they are genuinely hard — not because the answer is unclear,
but because real people are in real pain.

This lesson is about holding two things together: **truth** and **compassion**. Jesus came
"full of grace and truth" (John 1:14). If we give truth without grace, we sound harsh and
uncaring. If we give grace without truth, we abandon the most vulnerable person in the
situation. We need both.

## The Hard Cases, Answered with Truth and Compassion

Remember the key question from the first lesson: **what is the unborn?** Hard cases are
heartbreaking, but they don't change the answer. Still, how you answer matters as much as what
you say.

**Rape.** Rape is a horrific crime. The woman needs protection, care, counselling, and justice
against her attacker. Begin there, sincerely. Then gently point out that the child is not the
criminal. Scripture says children should not be put to death for the sins of their fathers
(Deuteronomy 24:16). Would we end the life of a two-year-old because she was conceived in
violence? If not, then the circumstances of conception don't change her value. Abortion cannot
undo the assault; it adds a second victim. It's also worth knowing that pregnancies from rape
are a small fraction of all abortions — surveys typically put them around one percent or less
— so don't let this case define the whole debate. But never use that fact to brush a real
woman's pain aside.

**The life of the mother.** Sometimes a mother's life is genuinely at risk. The most common
example is an **ectopic pregnancy**, where the embryo implants outside the womb (usually in a
fallopian tube). The baby cannot survive, and without treatment the mother is likely to die.
Pro-life ethicists widely agree that doctors should act to save the mother — the life that can
be saved. (Christians differ somewhat on which medical methods are best, but not on saving the
mother.) Later in pregnancy, when complications arise, doctors can often deliver the baby early
and try to save both. **The goal is to save life, not to take it.**

**Disability.** Prenatal tests can reveal conditions like Down syndrome or serious heart
defects. Families facing this need enormous support. But our value doesn't depend on our
abilities. We don't end the lives of disabled people outside the womb, and many live full,
joyful lives. Prenatal diagnoses are also sometimes mistaken. For conditions where a baby will
likely live only a short time, **perinatal hospice** lets parents love, hold, and name their
child for the time they have.

**Poverty.** Financial fear is one of the most common reasons women give for abortion. It is a
real burden. But we don't solve poverty by ending the lives of poor children. Instead, the
church must help carry the load — with money, housing, jobs, childcare, and friendship.

## Keeping Hard Cases in Perspective

Hard cases are often raised as though they settle the whole debate. They don't. Even if every
hard case were granted, they would not justify abortion for any reason at any time — and the
vast majority of abortions happen for other reasons.

A gentle question can help: *"Suppose I agreed with you about that case. Would you then agree
to protect unborn children in all the other cases?"* Pro-life speakers like Scott Klusendorf
have used this kind of question to show whether the hard case is the real issue, or whether
the person's objection is really to protecting the unborn at all. Ask it kindly, not as a trap.

At the same time, never treat a hard case as merely a debating point. Behind every "what if"
there may be a real woman — perhaps the person you're talking to. If you sense that someone is
speaking from experience, stop arguing and start listening. You can return to the argument
another day. You may not get another chance to show that you care.

## Speak as If Someone in the Room Has Had an Abortion

Because someone probably has. Abortion is common, and many women and men sitting in church
carry its memory in silence. How we speak about abortion will either drive them further into
hiding or draw them toward the cross.

So avoid harsh labels and angry tones. Speak of the wrongness of abortion clearly, but always
in the same breath speak of the **forgiveness** available in Christ. Remember too that **men**
are involved — fathers who pressured, paid for, or simply didn't stop an abortion. They also
need to hear the gospel.

## Grace, Not Shame

For someone who has had an abortion, the problem is usually not that they don't know it was
wrong. It's that they can't believe God could forgive them.

This is where the gospel shines. **1 John 1:9** promises that when we confess our sins, God is
faithful and just to forgive and to **cleanse us from all unrighteousness** — not most, all.
Notice it says God is **just** to forgive. That's because Jesus has already paid the penalty.
God isn't overlooking the sin; He has dealt with it at the cross.

Scripture is full of people who took life and were forgiven:

- **Moses** killed an Egyptian — and God made him the leader of His people.
- **David** arranged Uriah's death to cover his adultery — and wrote Psalm 51 in repentance.
- **Paul** approved the killing of Christians — and became the great apostle of grace.

> Shame says, "You are what you did." The gospel says, "You are who Christ has made you." In
> Christ, the verdict is **no condemnation** (Romans 8:1).

Healing may take time. Grief is appropriate — it's right to mourn a child. Many post-abortive
men and women find help in church-based recovery groups, where they can grieve, receive God's
forgiveness, and even name the child they lost. Your role is to be a safe friend who points
them, again and again, to Jesus.

## Practical Care

Being pro-life isn't just about arguments or laws. It's about people. Here are ways to show it:

- **Pregnancy resource centres** offer free ultrasounds, counselling, baby supplies, and
  support to women facing unplanned pregnancies. Volunteer, donate, or pray for one near you.
- **Adoption and foster care** give children families and give mothers hope.
- **Your church** can provide meals, childcare, rides, and friendship for single mothers.
- **Your home** can be a place where a frightened young woman finds a listening ear.

When the world says, "You're on your own," the church should say, "We're here, and we'll help."

## Keeping the Gospel Central

Hard cases and political debates can feel like the whole issue. They aren't. The deepest need
of every person involved — the mother, the father, the doctor, the activist, and you — is to be
reconciled to God. Abortion is a symptom of a deeper problem: sin. And the answer to sin is the
cross. Every pro-life conversation should, where possible, point people toward the One who
gives life and forgives sin.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise 1 John 1:9** — you may need it for a friend sooner than you think.
2. **Practise answering the rape case** out loud, beginning with compassion before moving to
   truth.
3. **Find your nearest pregnancy resource centre** and learn what help it offers.
4. **Pray for post-abortive men and women** in your church, that they would find healing and
   grace.
5. **Check your tone** — next time abortion comes up, ask yourself: "Would a hurting person
   feel safe hearing me talk?"
