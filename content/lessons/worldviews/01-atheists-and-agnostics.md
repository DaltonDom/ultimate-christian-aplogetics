  - q: "If God made everything, who made God?"
    a: >-
      Good question — but the argument isn't "everything needs a cause." It's "everything
      that *begins to exist* needs a cause." The universe began; God, by definition, did
      not. An eternal being doesn't need a maker any more than a circle needs corners.
      Asking who made God is like asking who the bachelor's wife is.
  - q: "Religion has caused so much harm in the world."
    a: >-
      Agree where it's true — people have done terrible things in God's name, and Jesus
      condemned hypocrisy more strongly than any atheist does. But the abuse of something
      doesn't prove it false. And notice: to call something "harmful" or "evil," you need a
      real standard of good. Where does that standard come from if there's no God?
  - q: "I'm agnostic — nobody can really know if God exists."
    a: >-
      That's a big claim — how do you know nobody can know? Most agnostics actually mean
      "I don't know yet," which is honest. Then the important question is: are you
      looking? If God has made Himself known in creation, in conscience, and in Jesus, then
      the question isn't whether it's knowable but whether we're willing to look.

Your coworker leans back in his chair and says, *"I'm an atheist. I just follow the
evidence."* Your heart starts racing. You wonder if you need a degree in physics and
philosophy before you can say anything at all.

Take a breath. You don't need to win a debate. You need to **understand the person in
front of you**, ask a few good questions, and remember something the Bible makes very
clear: every atheist was made in the image of God, has a conscience that knows right from
wrong, and will one day stand before the God they currently doubt.

## What They Actually Believe

"Atheist" doesn't mean the same thing to everyone, so start by finding out which kind of
unbeliever you're talking to.

- **"Lack of belief" atheism** (sometimes called *weak* or *negative* atheism): *"I'm not
  convinced God exists."* This person isn't claiming to prove there's no God; they're
  simply withholding belief.
- **"Denial" atheism** (*strong* or *positive* atheism): *"There is no God."* This is a
  claim about reality, and — like any claim — it needs reasons.
- **Agnosticism** (from a Greek word meaning "not knowing"): *"I don't know whether God
  exists."* A *soft* agnostic says, "I don't know yet." A *hard* agnostic says, "No one can
  know."
- **Apathy** (sometimes jokingly called *apatheism*): *"I don't really care whether God
  exists."* For many people today, God just isn't a live question.

Most atheists and agnostics you meet aren't angry or hostile. Many value **reason,
science, honesty, and kindness**, and try to live good lives without religion (a view
often called *secular humanism*). A surprising number grew up in church and walked away
after hard questions went unanswered, or after being hurt by Christians. If you ask, they
will usually tell you their story — and their story tells you where to begin.

## Common Ground

You share more with an atheist than you might think. You can honestly agree that:

- **Truth matters**, and we should believe things for good reasons.
- **Evidence matters.** Christianity isn't blind faith; it makes claims about history
  that can be examined (see the Apologetics module).
- **Science is a gift.** Many founders of modern science, such as Kepler, Boyle, and
  Newton, were believers who studied nature because they expected a rational Creator to
  have made an orderly world.
- **Religious hypocrisy is ugly.** Jesus was harder on religious hypocrites than anyone.
- **Suffering is a real problem** that deserves a real answer, not a slogan.

Starting here lowers defences. It shows you're not an enemy, just a fellow human being
thinking about the biggest question there is.

## The Heart Behind Unbelief

Here's something important: the Bible doesn't describe unbelief as purely an
information problem. Romans 1:18-20 says God's "eternal power and divine nature" are
clearly seen in creation, and that people **"suppress the truth"** — they push it down,
like holding a beach ball under water.

That doesn't mean every atheist is lying or secretly knows they're wrong. Many are
sincere. But it does mean that **intellectual reasons are rarely the whole story**. It's
common to see three layers:

1. **The head:** real questions about evidence, science, or suffering.
2. **The heart:** pain, disappointment, or bad experiences with Christians or church.
3. **The will:** an honest reluctance to have Someone else in charge of our lives.

Answer the head honestly, listen to the heart kindly, and gently ask about the will.

This is also why **the Law still applies**. An atheist doesn't escape God's standard by
not believing in it, any more than someone escapes gravity by not believing in it before
stepping off a roof. Romans 2:15 says God's Law is written on every heart, and conscience
bears witness. So even if you never settle every scientific question, you can still ask:
*"Have you ever lied? Stolen? Hated someone?"* Their conscience already knows the answer.

> An atheist's biggest problem isn't that they lack enough information about God. It's
> that, like all of us, they've sinned against Him — and believing in God wouldn't fix
> that by itself either. Even demons believe God exists (James 2:19).

## Questions for Atheists and Agnostics

Questions do what statements can't: they help people think without feeling attacked
(see Greg Koukl's Columbo tactic in the Tactics module). Here are some of the best:

1. **"What do you mean by atheist — no God, or not convinced?"** Clarifies the claim and
   who carries the burden of proof.
2. **"If God existed, what evidence would convince you?"** Reveals whether any evidence
   could count — and sometimes shows that evidence isn't the real barrier.
3. **"Where do you think morality comes from?"** If right and wrong are real, not just
   preferences, where does that standard come from?
4. **"Why is there something rather than nothing?"** Opens the door to the universe's
   beginning and its cause.
5. **"Were you always an atheist? What changed?"** Invites their story, and often
   uncovers hurt you can respond to with compassion.
6. **"On a scale of one to ten, how sure are you there is no God?"** Almost nobody says
   ten. That opens the door: *"So there's a chance you could be wrong?"*
7. **"If Christianity turned out to be true, would you want to follow Jesus?"** Gently
   probes the will, not just the head.
8. **"What do you think happens when we die?"** Brings the conversation from theory to
   something personal and urgent.

You don't need to use them all. Pick one or two, then listen carefully to the answer.

## Sharing the Gospel with an Atheist

You don't have to win every argument before you share the gospel. A useful pattern is:

- **Answer honestly and briefly.** If they raise a real question, give a clear, short
  answer — and admit it if you don't know. ("Great question. I'm not sure, but I'll look
  into it.")
- **Then pivot to the conscience.** Something like: *"Can I ask you a different kind of
  question?"* Then walk through a few of the Ten Commandments using the Good Person Test.
- **Bring it to the cross.** Once they see they've broken the standard they themselves
  believe in, explain that God is just and must punish sin, but that Jesus took the
  punishment in our place and rose from the dead. That resurrection is itself a historical
  claim they can investigate.
- **Call for a response.** Encourage them to repent and trust Christ — and, if they're
  not ready, to read one of the Gospels honestly, asking God to show Himself if He's there.

Remember: arguments can remove obstacles, but only the Holy Spirit gives new life. Your
job is to be faithful, clear, and kind.

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**

- Ask what they mean before you respond.
- Thank them for honest questions and take them seriously.
- Admit what you don't know.
- Share your own story of coming to faith.
- Keep coming back, gently, to the conscience and the cross.

**Don't:**

- Say, "You know God exists — you're just in denial." Let Scripture give you confidence,
  not ammunition.
- Mock, sneer, or call them a fool, even when they mock you.
- Pretend faith and evidence are enemies.
- Try to answer every objection in one conversation.
- Forget to pray. Only God opens blind eyes (2 Corinthians 4:4-6).

5. **Pray by name** for one atheist or agnostic you know, asking God to open their eyes.
