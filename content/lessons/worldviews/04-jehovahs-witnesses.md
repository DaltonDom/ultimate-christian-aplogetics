      That's true — and neither is the word "Bible," yet we both use it happily. The question isn't whether the word
      appears but whether the teaching does. The Bible teaches there is one God (Isaiah
      43:10), that the Father is God, the Son is God (John 1:1; 20:28), and the Holy Spirit
      is God and a person (Acts 5:3-4). "Trinity" is just a one-word summary of those truths.
  - q: "A loving God would never torment people forever in hellfire."
    a: >-
      I understand why that troubles you — it should sober all of us. But Jesus Himself
      spoke of "eternal punishment" alongside "eternal life" (Matthew 25:46), and taught
      about judgment more than anyone else in the Bible. (Some evangelicals believe the
      lost are ultimately destroyed rather than punished forever, but all agree that
      judgment is real and terrible.) The good news is that no one has to face it: Jesus
      took the punishment for sinners so we could be forgiven. That's the question that
      matters most — have you been forgiven?
  - q: "Jesus said, 'The Father is greater than I am' (John 14:28)."
    a: >-
      Yes — in His role as the Son who became a man and humbled Himself (Philippians 2:6-8),
      the Father was greater in position. But "greater" isn't the same as "better" or
      "different in nature." A king is greater in position than his son, yet they share the
      same human nature. Jesus is equal in nature to the Father while submitting to Him in role.
  - q: "Jesus is called the firstborn of creation, so He must be created."
    a: >-
      In the Bible, "firstborn" often means pre-eminent, the one with rights and honour — not
      the first one born. God calls David His "firstborn" (Psalm 89:27), though he was the
      youngest son. And Colossians 1:16 goes on to say all things were created by Him. (The
      New World Translation adds the word "other" — "all other things" — which isn't in the
      Greek text.)

It's Saturday morning. Through the window you see two neatly dressed people with a
magazine and a tablet, walking up to your door. You know who they are. Most people hide,
pretend not to be home, or say "No thanks" through a crack in the door.

But think about it: **people who want to talk about the Bible are coming to you.** Few
Christians get evangelism opportunities that easy. This lesson will help you welcome
them, understand them, and ask questions that point them to the real Jesus.

## What They Actually Believe

Jehovah's Witnesses are a worldwide organisation of more than eight million active members,
led by the **Governing Body** in the United States and served by the **Watch Tower
Bible and Tract Society**. The movement grew out of Charles Taze Russell's Bible Student
movement in the late 1800s. A Witness would recognise these as core beliefs:

- **Jehovah is the only true God** and the Almighty. Using His name, *Jehovah*, is very
  important to them.
- **Jesus is God's Son but not God.** He was Jehovah's first creation and, before coming to
  earth, was **Michael the archangel**. Through Him, Jehovah created everything else. He
  died on a *torture stake* (not a cross) as a **ransom** for sin, and was raised as a
  spirit being.
- **The Holy Spirit is not a person** but God's *active force*, like power in action.
- **The Trinity is false**, a later pagan-influenced teaching.
- **Only 144,000 go to heaven.** These are *anointed* Christians who will rule with Christ.
  Most Witnesses belong to the **"great crowd"** or **"other sheep,"** hoping to live
  forever on a restored paradise earth.
- **There is no eternal hell.** The soul isn't immortal, and the wicked simply cease to
  exist.
- **God's Kingdom began ruling in heaven in 1914**, and Armageddon, when God will destroy
  the wicked, is near.
- Witnesses avoid blood transfusions, don't celebrate birthdays or Christmas, stay
  politically neutral, and see preaching (at doors, and increasingly with public literature
  carts) as a central part of faithfulness.

Their Bible is the **New World Translation** (NWT), produced by the organisation. They
typically study it with Watchtower publications, and trust the Governing Body as God's
channel of teaching today.

## Common Ground

Witnesses are often **more zealous and disciplined** than many Christians. You can honestly
agree that:

- The **Bible is God's Word** and should be read and obeyed.
- God has a personal name (the LORD in most English Bibles translates it).
- **Jesus died as a ransom** for sinners and was raised from the dead.
- God will one day **end suffering** and make all things new (Revelation 21:4).
- Christians should live **clean, moral lives** and share their faith boldly.

## The Differences That Matter for Salvation

Two issues matter most.

**1. Who Jesus is.** If Jesus is a created being, then Christianity worships a creature.
If He is God, then denying His deity means missing the true Jesus. Jesus Himself said,
"unless you believe that I am he you will die in your sins" (John 8:24). The Bible shows
Jesus is God:

- **He created everything.** Isaiah 44:24 says Jehovah made all things *alone, by
  Himself*. Yet John 1:3 and Colossians 1:16 say all things were made through Jesus.
- **He is called God.** John 1:1, John 20:28, Titus 2:13, and Hebrews 1:8.
- **He is not an angel.** Hebrews 1:5-6 says God never called an angel His Son, and tells
  all God's angels to worship the Son.
- **He shares God's titles.** Jehovah is "the first and the last" (Isaiah 44:6); so is
  Jesus (Revelation 1:17; 22:13).

**A word about John 1:1.** The NWT says "the Word was a god," arguing that because the
Greek word for God (*theos*) has no "the" in front of it, it should be translated "a god."
You don't need to be a Greek scholar to respond. In the same chapter, *theos* appears
without "the" several more times (for example, verses 6, 12, 13, and 18), and the NWT
simply translates those as "God." Most Greek scholars, whatever their beliefs, read John 1:1
as saying the Word has the full nature of God. And the bigger problem is theological: the
Bible knows only one true God and many false ones. A "second god" beside Jehovah fits
neither category.

**2. How we're saved.** Witnesses believe Jesus' ransom makes salvation *possible*, but
they must prove faithful — through obedience, preaching, and loyalty to Jehovah's
organisation — to survive Armageddon. So most don't have **assurance** of eternal life.
The Bible offers salvation as a completed gift received by faith (Ephesians 2:8-9), which
we can *know* we have (1 John 5:13).

> An illustration: imagine someone pays your whole debt, but you're told to keep making
> payments just in case it didn't cover everything. You'd never rest. That's how many
> sincere Witnesses live. The gospel says: it is finished (John 19:30).

## Questions for Jehovah's Witnesses

Use their Bible whenever possible — the NWT still contains many clear passages about
Jesus.

1. **"Who do you say Jesus is?"** The most important question, and a good way to listen first.
2. **"Isaiah 44:24 says Jehovah made everything by Himself. John 1:3 says everything was
   made through Jesus. How do those fit together?"**
3. **"In John 20:28, Thomas called Jesus 'my God.' Why didn't Jesus correct him?"**
4. **"If Jesus is 'a god,' is He a true god or a false god?"** Isaiah 43:10 says there is
   no other god before or after Jehovah.
5. **"Hebrews 1:6 says all God's angels should worship the Son. If Jesus is Michael, is
   Michael an angel told to worship himself?"**
6. **"Can a force be lied to or grieved?"** (Acts 5:3-4; Ephesians 4:30)
7. **"Are you sure you'll live forever with God? What would you need to do to be sure?"**
8. **"Jesus said you must be born again to see God's Kingdom (John 3:3). Have you been
   born again?"** Watchtower teaching limits this to the 144,000, which can open an honest
   conversation.

## Sharing the Gospel with a Witness

- **Focus on the person of Jesus.** Everything flows from who He is. If He's God, then His
  sacrifice was infinite and fully sufficient.
- **Use the Law to show we can't earn it.** Witnesses take obedience seriously. Ask if
  they've kept God's commandments perfectly. If they're honest, they'll admit they
  haven't — which means they need grace, not more effort.
- **Share your assurance.** Tell them how you know you're forgiven, and invite them to
  trust Jesus directly as their Saviour and mediator (1 Timothy 2:5).
- **Play the long game.** Witnesses are taught to view outside Christian material with
  suspicion. One conversation rarely changes everything. Friendship, patience, and prayer
  matter.

## Being Kind at the Door

**Do:**

- Smile, thank them, offer a drink, and learn their names.
- Ask to read from their Bible.
- Stick to **one verse or topic** per visit.
- Invite them to come back.
- Remember that leaving can cost a Witness their family and friends. Be ready to be a
  friend.

**Don't:**

- Slam the door, mock, or call them names.
- Get lost in Greek grammar you don't understand.
- Bring up twenty issues at once.
- Take it personally if they won't accept your literature.
- Forget to pray for them after they leave.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise John 1:1** and learn where to find **John 1:3, John 20:28, and Isaiah 44:24**.
2. **Practise asking** the Thomas question warmly: "Why didn't Jesus correct him?"
3. **Prepare your assurance story** in two minutes: how you know you have eternal life.
4. **Decide in advance** how you'll respond the next time Witnesses knock.
5. **Pray** for the Witnesses in your area, by name if you know them.
