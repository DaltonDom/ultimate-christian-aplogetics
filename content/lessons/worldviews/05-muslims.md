Imagine sitting at a table piled with dates, lentil soup, and sweet tea. It's sunset during
Ramadan, and your Muslim friend's family has just broken their fast. They pray more often than
most Christians you know. They speak of God with deep reverence. They say they love Jesus.

What do you say?

Many Christians freeze, because they assume that talking about faith with a Muslim will mean
a heated debate. It doesn't have to. Your friend already believes in one Creator, in prophets,
in a Day of Judgment, and in Jesus. You have more to talk about than you think — and one
beautiful question that can open everything up.

## What Muslims Actually Believe

Islam means "submission" (to God), and a Muslim is "one who submits." Muslims vary a great
deal — Sunni and Shia, devout and secular, Arab, Asian, African, and Western — so always ask
your friend what *they* believe. But most would recognise this summary:

- **Tawhid** — the absolute oneness of God (Allah, the Arabic word for God, also used by Arab
  Christians). God has no partners, no equals, and no children. The worst sin is **shirk**,
  which means associating anything with God.
- **Prophets** — God has sent many prophets, including Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, David,
  and Jesus. Muhammad is the final prophet, the "seal of the prophets."
- **Scriptures** — God gave the Torah (Tawrat) to Moses, the Psalms (Zabur) to David, the
  Gospel (Injil) to Jesus, and finally the Qur'an to Muhammad. Most Muslims believe the
  earlier books were later corrupted, and the Qur'an is the final, perfectly preserved word.
- **Jesus (Isa)** — born of the virgin Mary, called the Messiah and a "word" from God, a
  worker of miracles who will return one day. But He is a prophet, not God, and not the Son of
  God. Based on **Qur'an 4:157**, most Muslims believe Jesus was **not crucified** — it was
  only made to look that way — and that God raised Him to Himself.
- **Judgment** — on the Last Day, a person's deeds will be weighed on the **scales**. Those
  whose good deeds are heavier, by God's mercy, enter Paradise.
- **Practice** — the Five Pillars: the confession of faith, prayer five times a day, giving to
  the poor, fasting in Ramadan, and pilgrimage to Mecca.

## Common Ground Worth Celebrating

Don't rush past what you share. You can say "I agree" often:

- There is **one God**, the Creator of all, who is holy, powerful, and merciful.
- God speaks through **prophets** and **Scripture**.
- Jesus was **born of a virgin**, is the **Messiah**, did real miracles, and will **return**.
- There will be a **Day of Judgment**, and sin is serious.
- Prayer, modesty, and devotion matter.

Starting here tells your friend you are listening, not just waiting for your turn.

## The Differences That Matter for Salvation

There are many differences, but three sit right at the heart of the gospel.

**1. Who Jesus is.** Christians believe the one God exists eternally as Father, Son, and Holy
Spirit, and that the Son became a man in Jesus. To a Muslim this can sound like shirk. Be
patient: many Muslims have been told the Trinity means God, Mary, and Jesus, or that "Son of
God" means God had a wife. Neither is what Christians believe.

**2. Whether Jesus died and rose.** If there was no cross, there was no payment for sin and
no resurrection. Paul says this is "of first importance." It is also one of the best-attested
facts of ancient history — even non-Christian writers mention Jesus' execution.

**3. How a person is made right with God.** In Islam, you hope your good deeds outweigh your
bad, and you trust God's mercy. But no Muslim can be certain of the outcome. In the gospel, you
are made right with God as a gift, because Jesus paid your penalty — and you can **know** you
have eternal life.

> Picture a set of scales. Put every good deed you've ever done on one side. Now put every
> lie, every lustful look, every flash of hatred on the other. Even if the good side were
> heavier, would a just judge simply ignore the crimes on the other side?

## Questions That Open Hearts

Questions are more powerful than lectures, especially with someone who has been taught that
Christians are confused. Try some of these, and really listen to the answers:

1. **"How can God be perfectly just and perfectly forgiving at the same time?"** This is the
   golden question. It honours their belief in God's justice and mercy — and only the cross
   answers it fully.
2. **"On the Day of Judgment, how confident are you that your good deeds will outweigh your
   bad?"** Most sincere Muslims will say, "Only God knows."
3. **"If a human judge let a guilty man go free because he'd done some good things, would
   that be justice?"**
4. **"The Qur'an tells Christians to judge by the Gospel (5:47). When do you think the
   Gospel was corrupted — before or after Muhammad?"** If before, why would the Qur'an point
   to it? If after, we have manuscripts from before his time to check against.
5. **"The Qur'an says no one can change God's words. Could people really corrupt the Torah
   and the Gospel God sent?"**
6. **"Why do you think the Qur'an calls Jesus 'the Messiah' and 'a word from God'? What does
   that mean to you?"**
7. **"Have you ever read the Injil yourself? Would you like to read Luke with me?"**
8. **"When you pray, do you ever feel close to God — like a child with a father?"**

You won't use all of these in one conversation. Pick one or two and let the conversation
breathe.

## Sharing the Gospel with a Muslim Friend

Your friend already believes God gave the Ten Commandments to Moses. That means you can use
the Law, as in the rest of this course, without it feeling foreign. Walk gently through a few
commandments — lying, stealing, coveting, lust — and ask whether they would be innocent or
guilty before a holy God. Most Muslims take sin seriously and will answer honestly.

Then bring in the **sacrifice**. Every year at **Eid al-Adha**, Muslims remember how God
ransomed Abraham's son with a "great sacrifice" (Qur'an 37:107). Ask: *"Why did God require a
sacrifice there?"* Then explain that the whole Bible builds toward one final sacrifice — the
Lamb that God Himself provides. Jesus didn't only teach about forgiveness; He paid for it.
And then He rose from the dead, proving His claims were true.

Finally, speak about **assurance**. Many Muslims have never heard that a person can know they
are forgiven. That isn't arrogance — it's trust in what Someone else has done.

## Respectful Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**

- **Accept hospitality** and offer it. Friendship matters deeply in most Muslim cultures, and
  many Muslims have never been invited into a Christian home.
- **Treat your Bible with honour** — don't put it on the floor or scribble carelessly in it
  while they watch. It shows you believe it is God's Word.
- **Pray openly** and talk about God naturally. Muslims often respect a person of faith more
  than a secular one.
- **Ask what they believe** rather than assuming.
- **Be patient.** Many Muslims who come to Christ do so over years of friendship, and may
  face real costs from family and community.

**Don't:**

- **Mock Muhammad or the Qur'an.** Insults close hearts and prove nothing.
- **Argue to win.** You may win the point and lose the person.
- **Be careless across genders.** In many Muslim cultures, deep one-on-one spiritual
  conversations are best had man to man and woman to woman.
- **Treat every Muslim as the same.** A secular Turkish student and a devout Saudi
  grandmother are very different people.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise Romans 3:26** — it is the answer to the golden question.
2. **Practise the golden question** out loud: "How can God be just and forgiving at the
   same time?" Then practise explaining the answer in under a minute.
3. **Learn a few basics** — tawhid, Isa, Injil, Eid al-Adha — so your friend sees that you
   have taken the time to understand them.
4. **Offer to read a Gospel together.** Luke is a great place to start.
5. **Pray** for a Muslim you know by name, and look for a chance to share a meal.
