Two young men in white shirts, dark trousers, and name tags are walking up your street.
You've seen them before. Most people hide or say, *"Not interested."* But these are
real young people, far from home, who have given up to two years of their lives to
serve what they believe is the truth about Jesus.

What if, instead of hiding, you invited them in, offered a cold drink, and asked them one
really good question? This lesson will help you do exactly that.

## What They Actually Believe

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (often called "LDS" or "Mormon" — though
the church now asks people to use its full name, and many members prefer "Latter-day
Saints") has around 17 million members worldwide. Here's what a faithful member would
recognise as their core beliefs:

- **The Restoration.** After the apostles died, the true church fell away (the *Great
  Apostasy*). In 1820, Joseph Smith saw God the Father and Jesus Christ in a vision (the
  *First Vision*), and was later called as a prophet to restore the true church.
- **Scripture.** The Bible is God's word "as far as it is translated correctly." They also
  accept the **Book of Mormon** (published 1830, described as an ancient record of peoples
  in the Americas whom Jesus visited after His resurrection), the **Doctrine and
  Covenants**, and the **Pearl of Great Price**. A living prophet leads the church today.
- **The nature of God.** The Father, Son, and Holy Ghost are **three separate beings**,
  united in purpose. Heavenly Father has a glorified body of flesh and bone. All people
  lived before birth as **spirit children** of Heavenly Parents. Jesus is the firstborn
  of those spirit children.
- **The plan of salvation.** Nearly everyone will be resurrected and receive some degree
  of glory. But the highest goal is **exaltation** — living forever as families in God's
  presence and becoming like Him. This requires faith in Christ, repentance, baptism,
  receiving the gift of the Holy Ghost, temple ordinances (including eternal marriage),
  and faithfully keeping covenants to the end.
- **Grace.** Latter-day Saints believe in the grace of Christ's Atonement. The Book of
  Mormon says, "it is by grace that we are saved, after all we can do" (2 Nephi 25:23).
  Many members today emphasise grace strongly and interpret this verse in different ways,
  so **ask your friend what it means to them.**

Latter-day Saints are known for strong families, high moral standards, service, and
generosity. They'd describe their faith as **the fullness of the gospel of Jesus Christ**.

## Common Ground — and Why You Must Define Terms

You'll share a lot of vocabulary. You both talk about God, Jesus, the Atonement, grace,
faith, repentance, being saved, and eternal life. You both value family, morality, and
service. That's real common ground.

But **the same words often carry different meanings**, which is why defining terms
(see Tactics) is essential here:

| Word | Historic Christianity | Latter-day Saint teaching |
|---|---|---|
| **God** | One eternal God, who has always been God; a spirit, in three persons | Heavenly Father, an exalted being with a body; one of three separate beings |
| **Jesus** | The eternal Son, who became man | The firstborn spirit child of Heavenly Father |
| **Salvation** | Rescue from sin and judgment, received by faith | Can mean resurrection (given to nearly all), or, in its fullest sense, exaltation |
| **Eternal life** | Knowing God forever, a free gift | Exaltation — the highest degree of glory |
| **Grace** | God's undeserved favour, not mixed with works | Divine help that makes up for what we can't do |

Ask simple questions like, *"When you say 'saved,' what do you mean?"* It prevents
people from talking past each other.

## The Differences That Matter for Salvation

Two differences are especially important:

**1. Who God is.** The Bible teaches there has only ever been one God. Isaiah 43:10 says
no god was formed before Him, and none will come after. Latter-day Saint teaching holds
that Heavenly Father is an exalted being, and that His children can become like Him
through exaltation. Members often read Isaiah as meaning there is no other God *for us to
worship* — so ask how that fits "before me no god was formed." There is a real
difference here about who God is.

**2. How we're saved.** The Bible teaches that eternal life is a **gift** received by
faith, "not a result of works" (Ephesians 2:8-9). Latter-day Saint teaching holds that
exaltation depends on faithfully keeping covenants and commandments. That often leaves
sincere members wondering whether they've done enough — and without the **assurance**
that 1 John 5:13 offers.

## The "Burning in the Bosom"

Missionaries will often invite you to read the Book of Mormon and pray about it, based on
a promise in Moroni 10:4 that God will reveal its truth by the Holy Ghost. Many members
describe this as a warm, peaceful confirmation — sometimes called a *burning in the
bosom* (from Doctrine and Covenants 9:8).

Take their experience seriously; don't mock it. But gently point out:

- **Feelings are real but not reliable guides to truth.** Jeremiah 17:9 says the heart is
  deceitful. Muslims, Hindus, and others also report powerful spiritual confirmations.
- **God tells us to test teachings against His Word** (Acts 17:11; 1 John 4:1). If a
  teaching contradicts what God has already said, no feeling can make it true.

## Questions for Latter-day Saints

1. **"When you say 'God,' 'Jesus,' or 'saved,' what do you mean?"** Clarifies terms before
   you go further.
2. **"How do you know when you've done 'all you can do'?"** The heart question —
   asked kindly, it often opens a real conversation.
3. **"Are you confident you'll reach the celestial kingdom?"** Invites them to be honest
   about assurance.
4. **"If you repent of a sin and later commit it again, what happens?"** Uncovers the
   burden of their view of repentance.
5. **"How do you understand Isaiah 43:10?"** Opens the conversation about one God.
6. **"If someone's prayers told them the Qur'an was true, how would you help them test
   that?"** Shows why feelings need an outside test.
7. **"What would Jesus have to do for you to be sure you're forgiven?"** Leads naturally
   to the finished work of Christ.

## Sharing Grace

Many Latter-day Saints are **working very hard** and carrying a heavy load. The gospel
you have is exactly what they need.

- **Use the Law to show that "all we can do" is never enough.** Jesus said to be perfect
  (Matthew 5:48). Ask if they've kept every commandment, every day. The Law shows that
  we need a Saviour, not a helper.
- **Share your own assurance.** Tell them how you know you're forgiven — not because
  you're good, but because Jesus paid it all.
- **Explain grace clearly.** Grace isn't God topping up our efforts. It's God giving
  eternal life freely to people who deserve the opposite (Romans 4:4-5; Titus 3:5).
- **Show that works are fruit, not root.** Christians obey because they're saved, not to
  get saved (Ephesians 2:10).

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**

- Offer hospitality and friendship.
- Use their preferred name for their church.
- Ask what they mean before assuming.
- Stick to one or two topics per visit.
- Tell them how Jesus gives you assurance.

**Don't:**

- Lead with shocking or obscure doctrines to embarrass them.
- Mock Joseph Smith, temples, or temple garments.
- Assume every member believes every historical statement their leaders have made.
- Argue to win — missionaries will usually (and wisely) leave if things turn hostile.
- Forget that leaving can cost a Latter-day Saint family and community. Be a friend.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise Ephesians 2:8-9** this week.
2. **Learn the definitions table** so you can explain how shared words differ.
3. **Practise asking** "How do you know when you've done all you can do?" warmly.
4. **Prepare your assurance story** — a two-minute account of how you know you're forgiven.
5. **Pray for the missionaries** you see, and plan to invite them in next time.
