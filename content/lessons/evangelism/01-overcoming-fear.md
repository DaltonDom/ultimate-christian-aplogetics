You know the moment. A coworker mentions a funeral, or a friend asks what you did at
the weekend, and the door to talk about Jesus swings wide open. Your heart speeds up.
Your mouth goes dry. And then you hear yourself say, *"Anyway, how about that game last
night?"*

Afterwards you feel a twinge of guilt. Why is it so hard to talk about the most
important thing in your life?

If that's you, you're in very good company. Many sincere Christians admit they rarely
or never share their faith. The good news is that fear can be understood, faced, and
overcome, not by becoming a different personality, but by seeing things the way God
sees them.

## The Real Reasons We Stay Quiet

Be honest with yourself. When you don't share, what's usually going on inside? For most
of us it's one of these:

- **Fear of rejection.** *"They'll think I'm weird or pushy."*
- **Fear of not knowing enough.** *"What if they ask something I can't answer?"*
- **Fear of losing a relationship.** *"It'll make things awkward at work or at home."*
- **Feeling unqualified.** *"I'm not good enough to talk about God."*
- **Simple forgetfulness.** *"I'm busy, and it never crosses my mind."*

Look closely and most of these share a root. The Bible calls it the **fear of man**,
caring more about what people think of *us* than about what happens to *them*.

That's uncomfortable to admit. But naming it is the first step to beating it.

## The Fear of Man Is a Trap

Proverbs 29:25 gives a vivid picture: *"The fear of man lays a snare."* A **snare** is a
hunter's trap. It doesn't hurt when you step into it. It just quietly holds you in
place.

That's exactly what fear does. It rarely makes us deny Jesus out loud. It simply keeps
us still.

Think of Peter. He swore he'd die for Jesus, yet on the night of the arrest he denied
even knowing Him, not to a soldier but to a servant girl (Luke 22:54-62). A few weeks
later the same Peter stood before the rulers who had crucified Jesus and preached with
astonishing boldness (Acts 4). What changed?

- He had seen the **risen Christ**.
- He had been filled with the **Holy Spirit**.
- He and the other believers **prayed for boldness**, and God answered (Acts 4:29-31).

The same resources are yours. Paul told a timid young pastor that *"God gave us a spirit
not of fear but of power and love and self-control"* (2 Timothy 1:7). Feeling afraid is
not a sin. It's a normal human feeling. But fear is not meant to be in charge. **Courage
isn't the absence of fear; it's obeying God while you're afraid.**

## Love Is Stronger Than Fear

Here is the most powerful cure for the fear of man: **love for people**.

> Imagine walking home at 3 a.m. and seeing smoke pouring from your neighbour's house.
> The lights are off and they're asleep inside. Would you stand on the pavement
> wondering whether knocking would seem rude? Of course not. You'd hammer on the door
> and shout until they woke up. Nobody would call that pushy. They'd call it love.

Christians believe something far more serious than a house fire. The Bible teaches that
every person will one day stand before God (Hebrews 9:27), and that those who die in
their sins face His just judgment. If we really believe that, silence isn't politeness.
It's a failure of love.

God said something sobering to the prophet Ezekiel: a **watchman** who sees danger
coming and doesn't sound the alarm is held responsible (Ezekiel 33:6). We're not the
ones who save anyone, but we are called to warn and to tell them where safety is found.

It also helps to remember what the message *is*. You're not selling a product or
defending your ego. You're a forgiven sinner telling other sinners where forgiveness is.
And the **power is in the gospel itself** (Romans 1:16), not in your personality. You
don't have to be clever. You have to be faithful.

## What Usually Happens (and What If It Doesn't Go Well?)

Our imaginations are dramatic. We picture shouting, ridicule, or losing every friend we
have. In reality, most people are far more polite than we expect. Many are curious.
Some are quietly grateful that someone cared enough to ask.

What's the realistic worst case in an everyday conversation?

- A polite *"No thanks."*
- An eye-roll or an awkward pause.
- A disagreement, maybe a heated one.

That's it, most of the time. Now weigh that against what's at stake for the other
person. Put side by side, a moment of awkwardness looks very small.

And if you are rejected, remember two things. First, Jesus told us to expect it
(John 15:18-20), so it doesn't mean you've failed. Second, rejection of the message
isn't rejection of you. You're in the company of the prophets, the apostles, and the
Lord Himself.

## Small First Steps

You don't have to start with the hardest thing. Nobody learns to swim by jumping off a
cliff. Start in the shallow end and build from there.

1. **Pray daily for boldness and opportunities.** Name three people you'd love to see
   come to Christ. Prayer changes your heart before it changes theirs.
2. **Carry gospel tracts.** A tract is a short printed leaflet that explains the gospel.
   Many are eye-catching, like the novelty "million-dollar bill" tracts produced by
   Living Waters. Leave one with a generous tip, or hand one to a cashier with a smile:
   *"Did you get one of these?"* It takes five seconds and does the talking for you.
3. **Ask one question.** You don't need a full presentation. Just ask, *"Do you have
   any spiritual beliefs?"* or *"What do you think happens after we die?"* Then listen.
4. **Prepare a 60-second testimony.** What were you like, how did you come to see your
   sin and trust Christ, and what's different now? Keep the focus on Jesus, not on you.
5. **Go with a friend.** Courage is contagious. Many Christians find their first
   conversations far easier with someone more experienced beside them.
6. **Practise out loud.** Use the Skeptic Simulator drills in this course. Words you've
   already said out loud come much more easily when it counts.

Every time you take a small step and survive, the fear shrinks a little. It rarely
disappears completely, but it loses its grip.

## When Fear Wins (and Sometimes It Will)

Here's an honest word: there will be days when you chicken out. The moment comes, and
you let it pass. You'll drive home kicking yourself.

When that happens, don't wallow in guilt, and don't give up. Do what Peter did after his
denial: go back to Jesus. The risen Lord didn't write Peter off. He met him on a beach,
cooked him breakfast, and gave him his calling again (John 21:15-19). The same grace that
saves sinners also restores cowardly disciples.

Then turn the failure into a lesson:

- **What exactly was I afraid of?** Naming it takes away some of its power.
- **What could I have said?** Write down one sentence you'll use next time.
- **Can I go back?** Often you can. *"Hey, the other day you mentioned your dad's
  illness, and I didn't say what I really wanted to. Can I share something?"* Most people
  are touched that you remembered.

Remember, too, that evangelism isn't a performance you pass or fail. It's an overflow of
knowing Jesus. The more you think about what He has forgiven you, the more fear gives
way to gratitude, and gratitude loves to talk.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise Proverbs 29:25** using the flashcards, and pray it back to God when you
   feel afraid.
2. **Write down your top fear** about sharing your faith, then write a one-line truth
   from this lesson that answers it.
3. **Buy or print some tracts** and put a few in your wallet, bag, or car today.
4. **Take one small step this week:** leave a tract with a tip, or ask one person,
   *"What do you think happens after we die?"*
5. **Pray Acts 4:29 for yourself** every morning this week: ask God for boldness to
   speak about Jesus, who died for sinners and rose again.
