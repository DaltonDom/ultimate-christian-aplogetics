Jesus was tired. It was about noon, the sun was high, and He sat down beside a well in
Samaria while His disciples went to buy food. A woman came to draw water, and Jesus
said something so ordinary it's easy to miss: *"Give me a drink."*

A few minutes later they were talking about eternal life, her private sins, and whether
Jesus was the Messiah. By the end of the day her whole town was coming out to meet Him
(John 4:1-42).

He didn't start with a sermon. He started with **water**. That's the skill this lesson
is about: moving a conversation from the **natural** (everyday life) to the **spiritual**
(God, sin, eternity, and the cross).

## Learning from Jesus at the Well

Read John 4:7-26 slowly and you'll notice a pattern you can follow:

1. **He started with the natural.** A thirsty man asking for a drink. Nothing
   religious, nothing awkward.
2. **He crossed barriers.** Jews didn't normally speak with Samaritans, and rabbis
   didn't chat with women in public. Jesus cared more about her than about what others
   thought.
3. **He sparked curiosity.** *"If you knew the gift of God … he would have given you
   living water"* (John 4:10). She wanted to know more.
4. **He moved to the spiritual.** Ordinary water leaves you thirsty again; the water He
   gives wells up to eternal life.
5. **He touched her conscience.** *"Go, call your husband."* Gently, He brought her sin
   into the light, not to shame her but to show her need.
6. **He didn't chase rabbit trails.** When she raised a debate about where to worship,
   He answered briefly and brought the focus back to true worship and to Himself.
7. **He revealed who He is.** *"I who speak to you am he"* (John 4:26).

The pattern is simple: **natural → spiritual → conscience → Christ**. You can use it at
work, at the gym, in a queue, or online.

## Step One: Open the Door

Most conversations with strangers or acquaintances start with small talk. That's not
wasted time. It's how people learn you're friendly and safe. Some simple openers:

- **Learn and use their name.** *"I'm Sam, by the way. What's your name?"* People light
  up when you remember it next time.
- **Ask about their day, and mean it.** Then ask a follow-up question. Real interest
  stands out.
- **Notice what's in front of you.** The weather, the long queue, the dog they're
  walking, the book they're reading, the game on the TV.
- **Give a tract.** *"Did you get one of these?"* is one of the easiest openers there is.
  Many tracts carry a question on the back that does the work for you.
- **Offer a genuine compliment.** *"You've been really patient with that customer."*

None of these are tricks. They're simply ways of treating people as people. Be
**honest** from the start: if the conversation turns spiritual, don't hide that you're a
Christian. Bait-and-switch damages trust.

## Step Two: Cross the Bridge

The hardest moment for most Christians is the switch from small talk to spiritual
things. The secret is to have a few **transition questions** memorised, so you're not
searching for words when your heart is pounding. Here are some that work well:

- *"Do you have any spiritual beliefs?"*
- *"What do you think happens after we die?"*
- *"Do you think there's a heaven? If there is, do you think you'd go there?"*
- *"Do you think you're a good person?"* (This leads straight into the Good Person
  Test, the subject of the next lesson.)
- *"Has anything ever happened that made you think about God?"*
- After a tract: *"Did you get a chance to read the back? What did you think?"*

Then **listen**. Follow up with *"Why do you think that?"* or *"What makes you say
that?"* People love to be heard, and their answers tell you where to go next. (For more
on this, see the Tactics lesson on asking good questions.)

It often helps to ask permission, especially before a longer conversation: *"Can I ask
you a question I ask lots of people?"* Almost everyone says yes.

### Where to go with their answer

When you ask *"What do you think happens after we die?"*, you'll usually hear one of a
handful of answers. Each one has a natural next step:

- **"Nothing. You just stop."** Ask kindly, *"How sure are you of that? If you're wrong,
  what's at stake?"* Then: *"Can I ask, do you think you're a good person?"*
- **"You go to heaven if you're good."** This is the most common answer, and it's a
  perfect lead into the Good Person Test: *"How good do you think you have to be?"*
- **"I think we come back, like reincarnation."** Ask what they base that on, listen
  respectfully, and then ask, *"If there is a God who judges, how do you think you'd
  do?"*
- **"I don't know."** Honesty is a great place to start. *"Would you like to know? Can I
  tell you what convinced me?"*
- **"I'm a Christian."** Wonderful! Ask gently, *"What do you think gets someone into
  heaven?"* Some people use the label without understanding the gospel, and others will
  encourage you greatly.

You don't need to answer every idea on the spot. Your aim is to get to the conscience
and then to the cross.

## Everyday Moments Are Bridges

Life is full of natural doorways to eternal things. Train yourself to notice them:

- **Death or illness.** *"I'm so sorry. How are you holding up?"* Later, gently: *"Has
  it made you think about what happens after we die?"*
- **News of injustice or crime.** *"Do you think anyone ever really gets away with
  it?"* This opens the door to God's justice and Judgment Day.
- **Stress and worry.** *"Where do you find hope when life gets heavy?"*
- **Holidays.** *"What do you think Easter is really about?"* Christmas and Easter hand
  you the topic on a plate.
- **Big life moments.** A new baby, a wedding, a birthday. These are moments when people
  naturally think about meaning and the future.
- **Your own life.** When someone asks about your weekend, mention church naturally. It
  signals who you are and often invites a question.

> Paul did the same in Athens. He noticed an altar inscribed "to the unknown god" and
> used it as his starting point to tell the city about the God who made the world, who
> will judge it, and who raised Jesus from the dead (Acts 17:22-31).

## Reading the Room

Paul told the Colossians to *"walk in wisdom toward outsiders"* and to make their speech
*"gracious, seasoned with salt."* **Salt** makes people thirsty. Good questions make
people want to think more. A few pointers:

- **Care first.** If someone is grieving or distressed, comfort them before you ask
  anything. Sometimes the gospel conversation comes the next day.
- **Watch for signals.** Are they leaning in, asking questions back, or checking their
  phone and edging away? Respect a closed door; you can always leave a tract.
- **Mind the context.** At work, use breaks, follow company policy, and don't hold a
  customer hostage at the till.
- **Handle rabbit trails like Jesus did.** If someone jumps to a side debate, say
  *"Great question. Can I come back to that in a minute?"* and return to the main
  thing.
- **Don't force it.** A scripted conversation feels scripted. But practice is what makes
  it feel natural, just like any other skill.

Your goal isn't to win a debate or to get through a presentation. It's to get to the
point where you can talk about sin, the cross, and the resurrection, because that's the
living water people are really thirsty for.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise Colossians 4:6** and three transition questions from this lesson.
2. **Read John 4:1-42** and underline each step Jesus takes in the conversation.
3. **Pick one opener and one bridge** and use them with one person this week, even if
   it's just asking, *"Do you have any spiritual beliefs?"*
4. **Notice doorways** in the news, at work, and in your family, and jot them down. You'll
   be surprised how many there are.
5. **Practise the drills** out loud until moving from small talk to eternity feels
   natural.
