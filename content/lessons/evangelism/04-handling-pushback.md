You've just asked someone, *"Do you think you're a good person?"* and they've answered
with total confidence: *"Yes, definitely."* Three thousand years ago, a wise king
predicted exactly that: *"Many a man proclaims his own steadfast love, but a faithful man
who can find?"* (Proverbs 20:6).

Pushback is not a sign you've done something wrong. It's normal. More than that, it's
often a sign that the **conscience is stirring**, and people naturally push back when
something hits close to home.

This lesson gives you calm, friendly replies to the objections you'll hear most, so
you can keep holding up the mirror without turning it into a fight.

## Why "I'm a Good Person" Is So Common

Before answering it, understand where it comes from:

- **We compare ourselves with others.** Next to a serial killer, almost anyone looks
  good.
- **We grade ourselves on a curve.** We remember our good deeds and forget our sins.
- **We don't know how holy God is.** When Jesus was called "good," He replied that *no
  one is good except God alone* (Mark 10:18). Goodness is measured by Him, not by us.

So your aim isn't to insult the person. It's to **move the measuring stick** from other
people to God.

## Short Answers to the Most Common Pushback

Each of these follows the same shape: **agree with what's true, give a simple picture,
and ask a question** that brings them back to their own conscience.

### "I'm better than most people."

*What they mean:* "Compared to others, I'm doing fine."

*What you can say:* *"If you were caught doing 90 in a 30 zone, could you tell the
officer, 'But other people drive faster'? God doesn't compare us to each other. He
compares us to His Law."*

### "I've never done anything that bad."

*What they mean:* "My sins are small."

*What you can say:* *"The Bible says whoever breaks even one commandment is guilty of
breaking the Law (James 2:10). If you're hanging over a cliff by a chain, how many links
need to break?"* Then remind them of what they've already admitted: lying, stealing,
blasphemy, lust.

### "God will forgive me. He's good."

*What they mean:* "God is kind, so He'll overlook it."

*What you can say:* *"You're right that He's good. That's actually the problem. If a
judge let a guilty criminal go free just because he felt kind, would he be a good judge?
So how can a good God forgive you without being corrupt?"*

This one is exciting, because the person is halfway to the gospel. The answer to their
question **is the cross**: God is both just and forgiving because Jesus paid the fine.

### "I don't believe in hell."

*What they mean:* Often, "I don't like the idea," rather than "I've examined the
evidence."

*What you can say:* *"Can I ask why not?"* Listen. Then gently: *"If it's real, not
believing in it won't change it, just as not believing in a cliff edge won't stop you
falling. Do you think it would be right if murderers and abusers were never held to
account?"*

Most people have a strong sense that justice *should* be done. **Hell is God's justice**
applied to real wrongs. Jesus Himself spoke about it seriously and often. And you can add
honestly: *"I don't like the thought of anyone going there. That's why I'm telling you
how to avoid it."*

### "Who are you to judge?"

*What they mean:* "You sound self-righteous."

*What you can say:* *"You're right, I'm not your judge. I've broken every one of these
commandments too. I'm just asking what you think God would decide."*

People sometimes quote *"Judge not"* (Matthew 7:1). In context, Jesus was warning
against **hypocritical** judging, criticising others while ignoring your own sin. In the
same passage He tells us to deal with our own sin first so we can help others (Matthew
7:5). That's exactly what you're doing when you include yourself.

### "Christians are hypocrites."

*What they mean:* Often, "I've been hurt or let down by people who claimed to follow
Jesus."

*What you can say:* *"You're right. Some are, and I'm sorry if you've been on the
receiving end. I'm a hypocrite in plenty of ways myself. But Jesus isn't. He's the one
person who never failed to live what He taught. And the church is supposed to be a
hospital for sinners, not a museum of perfect people. Can I ask, would God find you
innocent or guilty?"*

Don't defend bad behaviour. Agree, apologise where it's right, and then bring the
conversation back to Christ and to their own standing before God.

## Pushback Is Often Progress

It's easy to think a conversation has gone badly because the person argued. But look at
what's happening. People rarely defend themselves against something that doesn't
matter to them. An objection often means the message has **landed**.

- **They're engaging.** Indifference is harder to work with than disagreement.
- **Their conscience is speaking.** Excuses are usually a sign someone knows they need
  one.
- **You're not responsible for the outcome.** Your job is to be clear and kind. Changing
  hearts is God's work.

So don't panic when you hear pushback. Welcome it. Every objection gives you another
chance to show the person their need and point them to the Saviour.

## When They Change the Subject

Sometimes the pushback isn't an argument; it's an exit. The conversation gets personal,
and suddenly they ask, *"What about the dinosaurs?"* or *"What about people who've never
heard?"*

- **Honour the question.** *"That's a great question."*
- **Park it.** *"Can I come back to that in a minute? I really want to finish this
  thought first."*
- **Return to the conscience.** *"So you've said you'd be guilty. Does that concern
  you?"*
- **Come back to it if they still want to.** Keep your promise. Some questions are
  sincere, and the Apologetics module will help you answer them.

Jesus did this with the woman at the well. When she raised a debate about where to
worship, He responded briefly and then brought the conversation back to Himself
(John 4:19-26).

## When It Gets Heated

Occasionally someone gets angry. Stay steady. A few principles:

- **Lower your voice, don't raise it.** *"A soft answer turns away wrath"* (Proverbs
  15:1).
- **Don't take insults personally.** Their anger is usually with the message, or with
  their own conscience, not with you.
- **Find something to agree with.** *"You're right that Christians have been hypocrites.
  I'm sorry that's been your experience."*
- **Never win the argument and lose the person.** Your goal is their soul, not the last
  word.
- **Know when to go.** If they're only mocking, or it's getting unsafe, end kindly:
  *"I appreciate you talking with me. Can I leave this with you?"* (The Tactics lesson
  on keeping your cool goes deeper.)

Remember that the person who shouts at you today may be the one lying awake tonight
thinking about what you said. Leave the door open.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise Proverbs 20:6** and the five short replies on the flashcards.
2. **Practise each reply out loud** with the Skeptic Simulator until you can say it
   calmly without notes.
3. **Choose one illustration** (the speeding driver, the corrupt judge, or the chain)
   and make it your own.
4. **Notice your own pushback** when God's Word confronts you. It will make you more
   patient with others.
5. **Keep aiming for the cross.** Every objection is a chance to explain why Jesus had to
   die, and why He rose again.
