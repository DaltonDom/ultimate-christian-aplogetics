Everything in the last few lessons has been building to this moment. You've asked the
questions. The person has looked into the mirror of God's Law and said, quietly,
*"Guilty… and yes, that concerns me."*

Now what?

Many Christians freeze here, or hurry through a jumble of Bible phrases. This lesson will
help you do three things well: **explain the cross simply**, **call for a real
response**, and **avoid pressure** that produces false conversions.

## Knowing When to Move to Grace

The question *"Does that concern you?"* is the hinge. Listen carefully to the answer.

- **If they're concerned**, their pride has been humbled. **Move to grace now.** Don't
  keep hammering the Law. A wounded person needs bandages, not more bruises.
- **If they're not concerned**, they're probably still trusting in their own goodness
  (*"God will understand"*). Gently ask *"Why not?"* and spend a little more time on
  Judgment Day and God's holiness. But don't argue them into fear. Leave them with
  something to think about, and pray.

This follows Jesus' own pattern: **Law to the proud, grace to the humble.**

## The Courtroom: Explaining the Cross Simply

You don't need a long sermon. You need a clear picture and the key facts. Here's a way
to say it in about a minute, in your own words:

> *"Imagine you're standing in court, guilty of serious crimes, with a fine you could
> never pay. The judge is good, so he can't just let you off. But then someone steps
> forward and pays the whole fine for you. Now the judge can let you go, and justice has
> still been done.*
>
> *That's what God did. We broke His law, and the penalty is death and judgment. But God
> loves us so much that He became a man in Jesus Christ. Jesus lived the perfect life we
> haven't lived. Then He died on the cross in our place, taking the punishment we
> deserve. He paid our fine in His life's blood. And three days later He rose from the
> dead, defeating death. That means God can forgive you completely and still be
> perfectly just."*

Notice what that includes:

- **Who Jesus is:** God in human flesh, not just a good teacher.
- **What He did:** lived without sin and died as our substitute (*substitute*, someone
  who takes another's place). That's 2 Corinthians 5:21, sometimes called *the great
  exchange*: our sin counted to Him, His righteousness counted to us.
- **The resurrection:** Christ didn't stay dead (1 Corinthians 15:3-4). This shows His
  payment was accepted, and it means He's alive to save today.
- **God's love:** not love for good people, but for guilty ones (Romans 5:8).

Because they now understand their guilt, the cross makes sense. That's what the Law was
for.

**Make it personal.** Tie the cross back to what they admitted a few minutes ago: *"You
told me you've lied, stolen, and used God's name as a curse word. Those are the very sins
Jesus was punished for. He took what you deserve so you could be forgiven."* Suddenly the
cross isn't a religious symbol. It's about them.

**Keep it brief and clear.** Resist the urge to cover every doctrine at once. Say the
essentials, then stop and ask, *"Does that make sense?"* Their questions will show you
what to explain next.

## Repentance and Faith: The Response God Asks For

Jesus summed up the response in two words: *"repent and believe in the gospel"* (Mark
1:15). Explain them simply.

**Repentance** means *turning*. It's a change of mind about sin that leads to a change of
direction. It's not cleaning yourself up first, and it's not just feeling bad. It's
agreeing with God that your sin is wrong and turning away from it, towards Him.

**Faith** means *trusting*, not just agreeing with facts. Ray Comfort popularised a
helpful picture:

> Imagine you're about to jump out of a plane. Believing a parachute works isn't enough.
> You have to put it on and trust it with your life. In the same way, it's not enough to
> believe facts about Jesus. You need to trust Him personally to save you from the
> judgment you'd face.

You can say: *"You can't earn this, but you can receive it. Turn from your sins, and
trust Jesus alone. Not Jesus plus your good works. Jesus alone."*

## What to Say When They're Ready, and When They're Not

**If they want to respond,** point them to God, not to yourself:

- *"You can talk to God yourself, right now or tonight. Confess your sins to Him, tell
  Him you're turning from them, and put your trust in Jesus to save you."*
- If they ask what to say, don't hand them a script to repeat. Point them to a real
  prayer of repentance in the Bible, such as **Psalm 51**, which David prayed after his
  sin with Bathsheba. Suggest they read it and then pour out their own heart to God in
  their own words.
- Offer to pray *for* them, there and then if it's appropriate.
- Give next steps: a Bible (start with the Gospel of John), a Bible-teaching church,
  and your contact details so you can follow up (see the next lesson).

**If they're not ready,** don't treat that as failure:

- *"No pressure at all. Can I ask what's holding you back?"* You may uncover a real
  question you can help with.
- Leave something in writing: a tract or a Gospel of John.
- Tell them you'll pray for them, and then actually do it.

The seed you planted may take months or years to grow. That's God's business.

## Avoiding Pressure and False Conversions

In the parable of the sower, some seed fell on rocky ground. It sprang up quickly with
joy, but had no root and withered when trouble came (Mark 4:16-17). A **false
conversion** is when someone makes a "decision" that looks like faith but isn't genuine
repentance and trust in Christ.

It's possible to produce these ourselves, with good intentions, through:

- **Hurrying** someone to "decide" before they understand.
- **Emotional or social pressure**: *"Everyone else is doing it."*
- **Making a prayer the goal**, rather than Christ.
- **Pronouncing someone saved** because they repeated some words.

A decision you talk someone into, someone else can talk them out of. Remember that
**salvation is God's work**: He opens hearts (Acts 16:14) and gives new birth (John
1:12-13). Our job is to explain the gospel clearly and call people to repent and
believe. God's job is to save. Let God's promises, not your announcement, give them
assurance.

Christians disagree about the best way to invite a response, especially on the use of
the "sinner's prayer" and altar calls. See the section on where Christians differ.

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise 2 Corinthians 5:21** and use the flashcards daily.
2. **Write out the courtroom explanation** in your own words and practise saying it in
   under 90 seconds, including the resurrection.
3. **Practise explaining repentance and faith** to a Christian friend using the
   parachute picture.
4. **Keep a Gospel of John or a tract with you** so you have something to leave with
   anyone who isn't ready yet.
5. **Pray before every conversation** that God would open hearts, because only He can.
