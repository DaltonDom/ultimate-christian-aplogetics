A newborn baby is a wonderful thing. But nobody delivers a baby, says *"Congratulations,
you're alive!"* and then leaves it on the hospital steps. New life needs feeding,
protecting, and loving.

The same is true when someone comes to Christ. And the same is true of *you*, the
Christian who wants to keep sharing the gospel for decades, not just for one excited
week after finishing a course.

This last lesson in the module covers three things: **caring for new believers**,
**finding outreach that fits your life**, and **staying faithful when it's hard**.

## Caring for New Believers

Jesus told us to *make disciples*, baptising them and teaching them to obey all He
commanded (Matthew 28:19-20). That's more than getting a decision. If someone trusts
Christ through you, or even just shows real interest, here's how to help:

- **Get their contact details** and follow up within a few days. A simple message
  like *"Been praying for you. How's John going?"* means a lot.
- **Get them a Bible** in a readable translation, and suggest they start with the
  **Gospel of John**, one chapter a day. Encourage them to ask two questions as they
  read: *"What does this show me about Jesus?"* and *"What should I do about it?"*
- **Teach them to pray** simply and honestly, like talking to a Father who loves them.
- **Connect them to a Bible-teaching church.** The first Christians *"devoted
  themselves to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and
  the prayers"* (Acts 2:42). Offer to take them and sit with them. Walking in alone is
  scary.
- **Encourage baptism**, the first public step of obedience for a new follower of
  Jesus. Point them to a pastor to talk it through.
- **Anchor their assurance in Christ, not feelings.** New believers often have ups and
  downs. Remind them that they're saved by what Jesus did, not by how they feel.
- **Be patient.** Growth takes time. Look for signs of a new heart, like a love for God's
  Word, a new sensitivity to sin, and a desire for God's people. Keep loving them if
  growth is slow.

The best follow-up turns a new believer into a **new witness**. Help them tell their
friends what's happened.

## God Gives the Growth

Paul described his ministry like farming: *"I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the
growth"* (1 Corinthians 3:6). This truth sets you free in two directions:

- **Free from pressure.** You can't make anyone believe. You're not responsible for
  their response. You're responsible for your faithfulness.
- **Free from pride.** When someone does come to Christ, you can't take the credit.
  Often you're simply one link in a long chain of people God has used.

> A farmer doesn't pull up his seeds each morning to see if they're growing. He plants,
> waters, waits, and trusts the process. Your conversations are seeds. Some will sprout
> next week, some in twenty years, and some you'll only hear about in heaven.

So how do you **measure success**? Not by numbers, prayers prayed, or arguments won.
Success in evangelism is **faithfully sharing the gospel in love and leaving the results
to God.**

## Outreach That Fits Your Life

There's no single right way to share the gospel. Here are the main avenues, and you'll
probably use several:

- **Tracts.** Easy, low-pressure, and they keep speaking after you've gone. Leave them
  with tips, hand them out in queues, and carry them everywhere. (Don't put them in
  letterboxes where local rules forbid it. In the United States, for example, it's
  illegal to put unstamped items in mailboxes.)
- **One-to-one conversations.** The Good Person Test at a park, a bus stop, or the
  gym. Many Christians go out regularly with a friend or a small team.
- **Open-air preaching.** Speaking the gospel publicly, as Whitefield and Wesley did in
  fields and marketplaces. It isn't for everyone and it takes training, but it can reach
  people who'd never enter a church. Go with experienced people and check local laws.
- **Online and social media.** Comment thoughtfully, post the gospel clearly, and
  answer honest questions by message. The same gentleness and respect apply behind a
  screen. Avoid endless comment wars; aim for private, honest conversation.
- **Family and coworkers.** Often the hardest mission field. Play the long game: live
  consistently, apologise when you blow it, pray for openings, and don't turn every
  meal into a sermon. At work, respect company policies and use breaks.

Christians sometimes debate whether to focus on relationships or on reaching strangers.
See where Christians differ below. Both have a place.

## When It Hurts: Rejection and Discouragement

You will face rejection. Jesus promised it (John 15:18-20). He also said something
remarkable: when people reject His messengers, they're really rejecting Him (Luke 10:16).
It isn't ultimately about you.

Discouragement is real too. After a great victory, the prophet Elijah was so exhausted he
asked to die. God didn't scold him. He let him sleep, fed him, and then spoke to him
(1 Kings 19:1-18). Sometimes the most spiritual thing you can do is rest. Some practical
help:

- **Debrief with a friend** after hard conversations. Laugh about the awkward ones.
- **Remember the seed.** A "no" today isn't a "no" forever. Many believers heard the
  gospel several times before they believed.
- **Pray for the people who rejected you.** It keeps your heart soft.
- **Keep your own heart warm.** The fuel for evangelism isn't guilt; it's gratitude.
  People who know they've been forgiven much love much (Luke 7:47).

## Staying Faithful for the Long Haul

Enthusiasm fades, but habits carry you. Build a simple rhythm:

- **Pray** daily for boldness and for specific people by name.
- **Plan** one regular outreach, like a monthly afternoon of tracts with a friend or a
  weekly aim of one gospel conversation.
- **Keep learning.** Review your flashcards, drills, and this course.
- **Stay accountable.** Ask a friend to check in: *"Have you shared the gospel this
  week?"*

*"Let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give
up"* (Galatians 6:9). Because Jesus rose from the dead, your labour for Him is never in
vain (1 Corinthians 15:58).

## Putting It Into Practice

1. **Memorise Galatians 6:9** and say it whenever discouragement hits.
2. **Choose one outreach avenue** from this lesson and put a date in your calendar
   this week.
3. **Prepare a follow-up kit:** a few Gospels of John, a list of local Bible-teaching
   churches, and a simple reading plan.
4. **Find an evangelism partner** and agree to check in weekly.
5. **Thank God for the gospel**, and remember that you're sharing the same grace that
   saved you.
